Burnaby Civic Pavilion

The Burnaby Civic Pavilion occupies a sloped site at the edge of an established park, mediating between a dense residential neighbourhood to the north and a restored riparian corridor to the south. The programme combines a branch library, community meeting rooms, and a covered outdoor market hall — functions that share a single unifying timber roof structure and benefit from proximity to one another.

The primary structure is a series of exposed glulam portal frames spanning the full depth of the building, their rhythm establishing the spatial cadence of all interior spaces. Cross-laminated timber panels form the floor plates and lateral shear system, leaving the primary framing legible from every vantage point. The roof cantilevers six metres beyond the south facade to shelter an intermediate zone between inside and outside — part circulation, part gathering, part weather protection for the market stalls below.

Thermal performance drove a number of envelope decisions: a continuous exterior insulation layer keeps the mass timber warm and dry, triple-glazed curtain wall sections face south and east for passive solar gain, and a ground-source heat pump system handles the balance of heating and cooling loads. The building is designed to operate at net-zero carbon in use, with embodied carbon well below industry benchmarks owing to the sequestration properties of the structural wood.

Material choices throughout defer to the timber structure. Polished concrete floors absorb and re-radiate solar heat. Untreated western red cedar lines the exterior soffits and will weather to a consistent silver-grey over time. Interior walls are either exposed CLT or simple white plaster — nothing competes with the warmth and grain of the structural wood above.
